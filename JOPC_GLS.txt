
Author Guidelines

The Journal of Popular Culture publishes articles that are well-researched, well-written, and that make a significant contribution to the field of popular culture studies. Please review recent articles/issues to develop a sense of the kinds of research TJPC publishes. Narrowly conceived articles as well as those that lack scholarly research, have an inadequate analytical framework, and are poorly written typically do not fare well in the peer review process.

Prior to submitting to TJPC, it is wise for prospective authors to familiarize themselves with our submission guidelines and the Editorial Board’s work to ensure that articles are a good fit for the journal. Popular culture studies are vibrant and broad, and scholars examine them using myriad approaches. While we value and seek to publish interdisciplinary, engaging, and wide-ranging research, it is important to note that some articles may be more suitable for disciplines such as literature, history, musicology, and cinema studies, among others. We especially welcome original articles that focus on new, previously unanalyzed subjects and forms that either contribute fresh insights, expand upon, or advance older conversations.

Prospective authors should write their articles in clear US English, in active voice, and in third person. The writing should be scholarly (avoiding colloquialisms and unsubstantiated claims), accessible, and geared toward the broadest possible audience. We respectfully request authors to be sensitive to the social implications of prose, avoiding explicitly discriminatory language as well as words/phrases with racist, sexist, homophobic, transphobic, and classist overtones. TJPC is an inclusive publication, and the Editor-in-Chief, Assistant Editors, and peer reviewers support and value academic freedom. At the same time, we ask prospective authors to be respectful of others and our readership. Note: Prior to submission, authors of social sciences articles should reformat their manuscripts for a humanities journal, using a more narrative voice and the Chicago Manual of Style (CMS).

Effective January 7, 2025, please submit articles to the Wiley Authors submission portal via the following link: visit https://authors.wiley.com/journal/JPCU.

Authors may check the status of their submissions at any time by logging on to submission.wiley.com and clicking the "My Submissions" button. For technical help with the submission system, please review Wiley’s Wiley Authors Author Help Documents or contact submissionhelp@wiley.com.

As part of the submission process, we require a title page with author’s contact information, a short abstract and an author(s) bio.

Essays must fall within 5000-to-7500-words, and must be double-spaced and in 12-point, Times New Roman font. We strictly adhere to the 7500-word maximum, and thus, articles must not exceed it. Further, the maximum word count includes all citations and endnotes. Please note that the 5000-word minimum should be of written article text. If a submission has fewer than 5000 words, we will immediately reject it and ask the author to develop the article further. Similarly, if a submission exceeds 7500 words, we will immediately reject it and request that the author edit the article to make it adhere to the word limit.

TJPC’s review process is ‘‘anonymized,’’ meaning that reviewers do not know the names/identities of the prospective authors whose research they are evaluating. Please ensure that articles are devoid of identifying information that reveals authorship.

TJPC receives hundreds of submissions each year and the team and peer reviewers work hard to move them through the processes as quickly as possible. The standard review time for all articles (including revised manuscripts) is 4-8 months. Please refrain from periodically emailing about the status of submissions before 8 months have passed. If you have a question about your submission, please get in touch with the editorial office at: tjpceditorialoffice@wiley.com.

The Decisions: After articles have undergone the review processes, the Editor-in-Chief will render one of the following decisions: Accept, Minor Revisions, Major Revisions, Immediate Reject & Refer, Reject & Refer, Immediate Reject—Do Not Transfer, & Reject—Do Not Transfer. For insight about these ratings, please review the following descriptions:

Accept: Original manuscript that advances the study of popular culture. It is well-written, well-researched, devoid of glaring grammatical and formatting mistakes. The article only requires light copyediting to further assess clarity and check for minor grammatical and formatting errors.

Minor Revisions: Original manuscript that advances the study of popular culture and is nearly publishable. The article is well-researched, but may warrant minimal expansion/clarification, an additional scholarly source, and or extensive copyediting to correct grammatical issues that hinder the clarity and flow. This cumulative work extends beyond light copyediting and minor formatting issues, and thus, requires a greater amount of work than the TJPC team completes. Prospective authors must sufficiently copyedit, revise, and correct the manuscript to raise it to the level of “Accept.”

Major Revision: Original exploration with the potential to contribute to popular culture studies. At this stage, reviewers generally require prospective authors to address a combination of issues that might include conceiving of a stronger thesis, further emphasizing and articulating the validity of the argument, explaining the stakes of the argument, incorporating additional research, and addressing grammatical and formatting issues.

Immediate Reject & Refer: Generally, articles are outside of TJPC’s scope but may be a candidate for publication in another Wiley journal. The articles must undergo the other journal’s peer review process.

Reject & Refer: Generally, peer reviewers evaluate the articles and find that they do not align with TJPC but may be a good fit for another Wiley journal. The articles must undergo the other journal’s peer review process.

Immediate Reject—Do Not Transfer: Generally, articles are out of TJPC’s scope and or formatted incorrectly. They do not undergo peer review.

Reject—Do Not Transfer: Based on the quality of the article, it is unpublishable.

Importantly, TJPC’s team lightly copyedits all accepted articles, correcting very minor issues such as awkward word choice, formatting errors, and highlighting missing citations. If an article requires more extensive copyediting to revise and correct substantive issues that include but are not limited to, awkward word choice, phrasing and sentence structure, passive voice, formatting issues, verboseness, and lack of clarity, the Editor-in-Chief will return the articles to prospective authors to thoroughly revise the article.

Manuscripts must neither have been previously published, nor should they be submitted for publication elsewhere while under review by The Journal of Popular Culture’s Editorial Board members. Prior to copyediting, authors may post early drafts of their essays in digital repositories, such as academia.edu. However, after publication, authors must wait for 24 months to place their JPC-edited articles in digital repositories or on websites.

For documentation, The Journal of Popular Culture follows The Chicago Manual of Style (CMS). Please keep use of explanatory endnotes to a minimum.

For punctuation, capitalization, hyphenation, and other matters of style, The Journal of Popular Culture follows the The Chicago Manual of Style (Chicago: University of Chicago Press).

It is essential that authors check, correct, and bring manuscripts up to date before final submission. Authors should verify facts, names of people, places, and dates, and double-check all direct quotations and entries in the Works Cited list.

We are happy to receive your artwork in digital format. Please save line artwork (vector graphics) as Encapsulated PostScript (EPS) and bitmap files (halftones or photographic images) as Tagged Image Format (TIFF), with a resolution of at least 300 dpi at final size. Do not send native file formats. More detailed information on the submission of electronic artwork can be found at: http://authorservices.wiley.com/bauthor/illustration.asp.

Authors of manuscripts accepted for publication must subscribe to The Journal of Popular Culture for at least one year at the time of acceptance. Subscription includes membership in the Popular Culture Association.

Before final submission, the author will be responsible for obtaining letters of permission for illustrations and for quotations that go beyond ‘‘fair use,’’ as defined by current copyright law.

Please note that unless specifically requested, Wiley Periodicals, Inc. will dispose of all hardcopy or electronic material two months after publication. If you require the return of any material submitted, please inform the editorial office or production editor as soon as possible if you have not yet done so.

Continuous Publication

Under a Continuous Publication model used at Wiley, journal articles are published directly into an online issue with their final citations as soon as they are ready. There is no issue curation and no issue pagination; articles publish when they have completed production and are not held for upcoming issues. The ability to publish an article online before its issue is completed provides faster publishing of articles with final citation details for the academic community.
Wiley's Author Name Change Policy

In cases where authors wish to change their name following publication, Wiley will update and republish the paper and redeliver the updated metadata to indexing services. Our editorial and production teams will use discretion in recognizing that name changes may be of a sensitive and private nature for various reasons including (but not limited to) alignment with gender identity, or as a result of marriage, divorce, or religious conversion. Accordingly, to protect the author’s privacy, we will not publish a correction notice to the paper, and we will not notify co-authors of the change. Authors should contact the journal’s Editorial Office with their name change request.
